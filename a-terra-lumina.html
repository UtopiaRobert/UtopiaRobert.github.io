Notes from Monchique

Monchique, Algarve, Portugal · 2024

A Terra Lumina

Four months in the Algarve

← Projects & Places

In 2024 Josie and I spent four months at A Terra Lumina, a small community in the mountains of Monchique, in Portugal’s Algarve. It became an important chapter in our lives — a summer of shared work, food, old stone buildings, gardens, water, forest, and friendships.

This is a photographic record of our time there: not a complete history of the community, but the places, people, work, and everyday details that stayed with us.

02

Among friends

Life together

What gives these photographs their meaning is the life happening inside the landscape: people working, resting, eating, talking, building, and making a home together.

Among the people who shaped our four months were Chris, a German permaculturist, builder and arborist; Julia, a gardener from Germany; Linda, the property owner from Finland; Tobias from Germany; and Dredds from Montenegro. Josie is from Cornwall, and I am from the United States. That mix of backgrounds was part of the character of A Terra Lumina.

03

Land, water & ruins

A living landscape

A Terra Lumina was shaped by the land as much as by the people living there. Forest, old terraces, ponds, paths, stone walls, and the ruins of older buildings all overlapped. Some structures remained as ruins; others had been adapted with timber, plumbing, plants, curtains, and improvised details into simple spaces for everyday life.

04

Food & garden

Meals began in the soil

Food appears throughout the photographs: vegetables in the garden, harvests carried in by hand, outdoor preparation, shared cooking, and meals that were part of the rhythm of community life.

05

Ducks

Building a place for ducks

The ducks came after the enclosure. First we built a protected space for them using curved metal hoops, pallets, mesh, and materials already available on the property. Like many projects at A Terra Lumina, the enclosure took shape through shared work and improvisation.

Then the ducks arrived.

Ducks can fit very well into a permaculture setting. They forage readily for slugs and snails, are comfortable around water, produce eggs, and generally do less scratching in established garden beds than chickens.

We also have video from the duck project. Later we can add one short compressed clip here without making the page heavy.

06

Making together

The Earth Oven

One of the larger projects was a new and distinctive Earth Oven — a clay-built pizza oven formed by hand on a stone base. The work brought several of us together, combining Chris’s permaculture and building experience with the materials, labor, and ideas available on site.

07

Four months

An important chapter

Four months — roughly a third of a year — was long enough for routines, relationships, and the place itself to become familiar.

Looking back, what remains most clearly is the texture of everyday life: work shared, food grown and prepared, old walls adapted rather than erased, water moving through the land, and long days lived mostly outdoors among other people.
